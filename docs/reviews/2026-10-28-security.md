# Security analysis, 28 October 2026

`/barry-security --fix` on `011e88c`, which is `main` at `adc389c` with the skill of mircowidmer/barry#15 merged in. The fix is on branch `security-2026-10-28`.

## 1. Summary

Analysed: the migrations as applied to the local stack (all 44), the commands, the clock and the outbox, the Edge Function `deliver`, Auth (`supabase/config.toml` including `[remotes.hosted]`, `scripts/hosted-auth.mjs`), the app under `src/`, the web build and `web/`, secrets in the full history, npm and Deno dependencies, the workflows, hooks, config plugins and the guides.

Checks run:

- `npx expo lint`: 2 errors, both `import/no-unresolved` on `src/components/trail-map-view.web.tsx` (`maplibre-gl/dist/maplibre-gl.css`, `@maplibre/maplibre-gl-leaflet`), which came in from `main` and are not security findings. `npx tsc --noEmit`: clean after `npm ci`. `npm test`: 24 suites, 167 tests passed. `deno check` and `deno test supabase/functions/deliver`: 31 passed.
- `npx supabase db advisors --local --type security --level info`: no issues. `npx supabase db lint --local --level warning`: only implicit `text` to `refusal` casts in the commands, not a security matter.
- Privilege queries on the local database, and attack probes with two throw-away accounts and the anonymous key (both accounts deleted afterwards).
- `npm audit --omit=dev` (9 advisories) and `npm audit` (13). A regex secret search over `git log -p --all` and unreachable objects (gitleaks is not installed).

Findings: 0 Critical, 1 High, 2 Medium, 5 Low. The access control of the database held against every probe: no Adventurer and no anonymous caller could read, change, start, move, check in or delete anything that is not theirs.

The three that matter most:

1. **SEC-1:** one account can make Barry send unlimited messages, with text it chooses, to any address and number. It costs money, carries phishing from Barry's sender, and can delay or block the Alerts of every other Adventurer.
2. **SEC-2:** an email confirmation link puts a live session into the browser's address bar and history on barry.support, and nothing revokes it.
3. **SEC-3:** the session and the cached Adventure sit in a plain SQLite file that iOS includes in device backups.

## 2. Threat model

- **Assets:**
  - The safety promise: an Alert goes out when a Deadline passes, and nobody but the Adventurer can stop it.
  - The live Trail, the destination and the Deadline.
  - The Profile, including medical notes, and the contacts' addresses.
  - The sessions.
  - The budget and reputation of the email and text message providers.
- **Boundaries:**
  - The phone and PostgREST, through the publishable key and the user's JWT. Only `public` and `graphql_public` are exposed, and pg_graphql is not installed.
  - The Postgres roles: `anon`, `authenticated`, `service_role` and the definers of `commands` and `private`.
  - `deliver`, which runs with `verify_jwt = false` and is gated by `DELIVER_SECRET` and the HMAC callback token.
  - Twilio and Resend.
  - The Trail Map page on GitHub Pages, which reaches `trail_map` with the token from the fragment, and OpenFreeMap for the tiles.
  - The confirmation page.
  - CI and the Pages deploy.
- **Attackers:**
  - Another Adventurer reaches PostgREST and every command.
  - An anonymous caller reaches `trail_map`, `deliver` and Auth.
  - An Emergency Contact holds a token.
  - Someone near the phone reaches its storage and backups and the URL schemes.
  - The supply chain reaches npm, Deno, the Actions and the tile server.

## 3. Findings

### SEC-1 · One account can make Barry send unlimited messages, with text it chooses, to any address and number

**High** · CWE-770, CWE-799 · OWASP API4:2023 Unrestricted Resource Consumption, API6:2023 Unrestricted Access to Sensitive Business Flows · **proven**

**Where:**
- `public.emergency_contacts` has no limit per account, and its phone check is `CHECK (phone ~ '^\+[1-9][0-9]{6,14}$'::text)`, which takes any E.164 number, satellite and premium ranges included.
- `adventures.destination`, `adventurer_profiles.name`, `medical_notes` and `emergency_contacts.name` are `text` with no length limit.
- `commands.save_adventure` takes any number of `p_contact_ids`.
- `private.enqueue_to_contacts` (latest in `supabase/migrations/20261016110000_one_message_per_address.sql`) enqueues one row per address.
- `public.claim_messages` takes rows `order by m.id`, so an Alert waits behind every earlier row.
- `supabase/functions/deliver/messages.en.ts:34` builds `subject: \`${name} is heading out to ${facts.plan.destination}\``.

**Attack path:**
1. The attacker signs up; the hosted project needs a confirmed address.
2. They insert 150 contacts in one request, with any addresses or numbers.
3. They save an Adventure with all of them and start it.
4. They check in, then start a new Adventure, again and again. Each Start sends a Heads-up and each Check-in an All-clear to every contact.

The probe proved every step against the local stack:
- 150 contacts were inserted.
- One Start enqueued `heads_up rows enqueued by one Start 150`.
- A 200,000-character destination was accepted and went out in a Heads-up subject (Mailpit: 400,598 bytes).
- A `+881…` satellite number was accepted.

**Gain:**
- A cost run-up on Twilio: international and premium numbers, with up to 1,600 characters per message.
- Spam and phishing to strangers from `🐶 Barry <help@barry.support>`, with the attacker's text and links in the subject and body.
- With enough volume, provider throttling (`429`, which is retried), quota exhaustion or suspension, and a queue that serves rows in id order. Any of these delays or blocks the Alerts of every other Adventurer (harm 1).

**Source:** OWASP API Security Top 10 2023, API4 and API6.

**Fix:**
- Limit the contacts per account and per Adventure, and the Starts per account and day.
- Bound the free text with `CHECK (length(…) <= n)`.
- Allow text messages only to the countries Barry serves (Twilio Geo Permissions plus a check on the number).
- Let `claim_messages` take Alerts first.

**Outcome:** skipped, rule 2. Every one of these changes what an honest Adventurer can do, or the spec. It is the author's decision.

### SEC-2 · An email confirmation link puts a live session into the address bar and history on barry.support

**Medium** · CWE-598, CWE-200 · MASVS-AUTH, OWASP ASVS V3 · **certain** that the tokens arrive; **likely** for how long they work

**Where:**
- `src/lib/supabase.ts` leaves auth-js on its default `flowType: 'implicit'`.
- `src/app/(app)/sign-in.tsx` signs up with `supabase.auth.signUp(credentials)`.
- The project's own test shows the landing URL: `src/lib/confirmation.test.ts:38` has `'https://barry.support/confirmed#access_token=abc&refresh_token=def&type=email_change'`.
- `src/app/confirmed.web.tsx` reads the fragment but neither revokes the session nor removes it from the address.
- `supabase/config.toml` sets no `[auth.sessions]` timebox or inactivity timeout.

**Attack path:**
1. The Adventurer confirms a sign-up or an address change in a browser, often on a laptop.
2. The URL with `access_token` and `refresh_token` stays in the address bar, the history and any synced profile.
3. Whoever reads it later (a shared computer, a synced browser, an extension, a screenshot) exchanges the refresh token at `/auth/v1/token?grant_type=refresh_token` for a session.

**Gain:** the account. The attacker can call `check_in` on an Active Adventure, which stops the Alert (harm 1). They can also read the contacts, the History and the medical notes.

**Source:** OWASP ASVS V3.1.1 (session tokens never in URLs), MASVS-AUTH.

**Fix:** on load, the confirmation page revokes that browser's session (`POST /auth/v1/logout?scope=local` with the fragment's `access_token`) and clears the fragment with `history.replaceState`, after reading what it needs. Alternatively, switch to `flowType: 'pkce'`.

**Outcome:** skipped, rule 4. It cannot be proven here: the local stack has email confirmations off, the page needs a browser, and the change must keep `confirmedWhat`, which reads the same address. It needs `/barry-frontend-verify`.

### SEC-3 · The session and the cached Adventure sit in a plain SQLite file that iOS includes in backups

**Medium** · CWE-312, CWE-922 · MASVS-STORAGE-1, OWASP Mobile Top 10 2024 M9 · **certain** for the storage; **likely** for how long a backed-up token works

**Where:**
- `src/lib/storage.ts:1-3` has `import 'expo-sqlite/localStorage/install'; export const storage = localStorage;`.
- `src/lib/supabase.ts:38` has `...(storage && { storage }),`.
- expo-sqlite keeps the store in `Documents/SQLite/ExpoSQLiteStorage` with no backup exclusion and the default file protection class.
- The same file holds `barry.inProgressAdventure` (destination, Deadline, contacts' addresses, last position), `barry.trailBuffer` and `barry.pendingCheckIns`.

**Attack path:** someone with an unencrypted Finder backup, a jailbroken phone or the victim's iCloud backup reads the file and takes the refresh token. Refresh rotation limits the window to the time until the app next refreshes in the foreground.

**Gain:** the session (Check-in and the data, as in SEC-2), or at least the destination, the contacts' addresses and the recent Trail.

**Source:** OWASP MASVS-STORAGE-1. I could not reach docs.expo.dev from this session to cite the Expo storage docs, so the storage claims rest on the installed module source.

**Fix:**
- Keep the session in the Keychain, through `expo-secure-store` with `AFTER_FIRST_UNLOCK_THIS_DEVICE_ONLY`, or with an AES key in SecureStore encrypting the value.
- Exclude the file from backup.

**Outcome:** skipped, rule 2. Moving the session to another storage is named there as the author's decision. Visible effect: a phone restored from a backup asks the user to sign in again.

### SEC-4 · A six-digit recovery code is valid for an hour, limited only per IP

**Low** · CWE-307, CWE-640 · OWASP ASVS V2.5, V2.7 · **likely**

**Where:** `supabase/config.toml` sets `otp_length = 6` (line 233), `otp_expiry = 3600` (line 235) and `token_verifications = 30` (line 210), with no `[auth.captcha]`. `[remotes.hosted]` does not override any of these, so `scripts/hosted-auth.mjs` pushes them to the hosted project. `src/lib/password-reset.ts` signs in with `verifyOtp({ email, token, type: 'recovery' })`.

**Attack path:**
1. An anonymous attacker who knows the victim's address requests a code.
2. They spread guesses over many IPs, 30 per 5 minutes per IP, against 10^6 codes.
3. They request a fresh code at most once a minute.

**Gain:** account takeover.

**Fix:** `otp_length = 8` or more, `otp_expiry = 900`, and captcha on recovery.

**Outcome:** skipped, rule 1 (likely: whether hosted GoTrue also limits attempts per user is not visible from here). The fix would also change the spec's "six-digit code … valid for an hour".

### SEC-5 · The Trail Map page uses maplibre-gl 5.24.0, whose HTML sanitizer can be bypassed (GHSA-jrc7-96c5-q579, critical in npm)

**Low** · CWE-79 · OWASP Mobile/Web A06 Vulnerable and Outdated Components · **likely**

**Where:**
- `package.json:30` has `"maplibre-gl": "^5.24.0"`, locked at 5.24.0. The advisory covers `<=6.4.0`.
- `src/components/trail-map-view.web.tsx:20` loads `'https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/liberty'`.
- maplibre writes the style's `attribution` through `innerHTML = DOM.sanitize(…)`.

**Attack path:**
1. Whoever controls what tiles.openfreemap.org serves puts a crafted `attribution` into the style or TileJSON.
2. It runs as script on barry.support while a contact has `adventure#<token>` open.
3. It reads the token and the Trail and sends them away.

**Gain:** the location and Trail of every Adventurer whose contact opens the map (harm 2).

**Fix:** upgrade to `maplibre-gl@^6.16.0`. `@maplibre/maplibre-gl-leaflet@0.1.4` accepts `^6.0.0`. A Content-Security-Policy meta tag on the page would limit the damage too.

**Outcome:** skipped, rules 1 and 6. It needs a compromised third party, and the fix is a major version, which the skill only proposes.

### SEC-6 · Anyone can make the confirmation page on barry.support show text they choose

**Low** · CWE-451 · OWASP content spoofing · **certain**

**Where:** `src/lib/confirmation.ts:11` has `return found.get('error_description') ?? found.get('error');`. `src/app/confirmed.web.tsx:31` renders it as `<p className="note">{landing.error}.</p>`.

**Attack path:** the attacker sends `https://barry.support/confirmed#error_description=Your Barry account is locked. Call +41…`. The genuine Barry page shows the text under "This link did not work". React escapes it, so there is no script, but the text is the attacker's.

**Gain:** credible phishing on Barry's own domain (harm 4).

**Fix:** map Supabase's `error_code` values to fixed texts in `siteText` and never render free text from the address.

**Outcome:** skipped, rule 2. An honest user with an expired link would then see Barry's wording instead of Supabase's.

### SEC-7 · `deliver` lets anyone start a run when `DELIVER_SECRET` is not set

**Low** · CWE-636 (not failing securely) · OWASP ASVS V1.4 · **certain**

**Where:** `supabase/functions/deliver/index.ts:98` has ``if (request.headers.get('Authorization') !== `Bearer ${Deno.env.get('DELIVER_SECRET')}`) {``. With the variable unset, the template becomes `Bearer undefined`. A probe with `Deno.env.get` on an unset name showed the comparison matches that literal header. The delivery-report route fails closed in the same situation: `importKey` throws `Key length is zero`.

**Attack path:** if the hosted project ever runs without the secret (a failed `scripts/hosted-secrets.mjs`, a renamed variable), an anonymous caller sends `Authorization: Bearer undefined` to `/functions/v1/deliver`. They start drain runs at will and read the run's `{ delivered, health }` answer, which tells them whether Barry's delivery is currently failing. A run only sends what is owed, so no message is forged.

**Gain:** small: a view of the operator's health signals, and runs at a time the attacker chooses.

**Fix:** refuse every request while the secret is missing.

**Outcome:** fixed in `94aea69`. The new `kick.test.ts` pins it. Against the restarted local Edge Runtime, `Bearer undefined` gets `401` and the real secret `200`.

### SEC-8 · A stolen session can change the password without the current one

**Low** · CWE-620, CWE-602 · OWASP ASVS V2.1.5 · **certain**

**Where:** `supabase/config.toml:229` has `secure_password_change = false`, and the hosted project gets this value too. The app's current-password check in `src/lib/account.ts` runs only on the phone.

**Attack path:** someone holding a session (SEC-2, SEC-3) calls `supabase.auth.updateUser({ password })` directly, and then the email change.

**Gain:** they turn a temporary session into a permanent takeover, and the owner is locked out.

**Fix:** `secure_password_change = true` under `[remotes.hosted.auth.email]`, and a reauthentication step in the app's password change.

**Outcome:** skipped, rule 2. It changes the password flow for honest users signed in for more than a day, and the change goes out only through `hosted-auth.mjs`, which rule 7 forbids.

## 4. Hardening notes

**Database:**
- Functions in `private` keep Postgres' default `EXECUTE` for `PUBLIC`, for example `enqueue_to_contacts`, `reconcile` and `record_position`. They are unreachable: `private` grants no `USAGE` to `anon` or `authenticated` and is not exposed. Revoking would make that explicit.
- Supabase's defaults let `anon` and `authenticated` select `net.http_request_queue` and `net._http_response`, which briefly hold the `Authorization: Bearer <DELIVER_SECRET>` header of `kick_deliverer`, and execute `net.http_post`. This is unreachable because `net` is not an exposed schema. Exposing it would leak the secret.
- `private.tick_error` keeps an `adventure_id` and an error text after the account is deleted.
- A message already being sent when its contact is removed still goes out with the latest Plan; the probe saw this. The spec's "gets no further messages" holds only for messages not yet claimed.

**`deliver`:**
- `DELIVER_SECRET` (`index.ts:98`) and the callback token (`delivery-report.ts:33`) are compared with `!==`, not in constant time (CWE-208). Over the network with HMAC-length values this is impractical to exploit.
- Text messages carry the whole text. A long destination or long medical notes can push an Alert past Twilio's 1,600-character limit; Twilio would then refuse it, so that contact gets no Alert by text. This is self-inflicted, but it falls on the safety promise. The limits in SEC-1 would cover it.
- Subjects carry the name and the destination verbatim. The providers take them as JSON fields, so header injection is unlikely.

**Auth:**
- `minimum_password_length = 6` and `password_requirements = ""` reach the hosted project.
- Leaked password protection is a dashboard setting I could not see.
- `email_sent = 30` per hour is shared by sign-ups to arbitrary addresses. Without captcha they can exhaust it and so block confirmations and recovery codes.

**The app:**
- Every iOS variant, production included, has `NSAllowsLocalNetworking` from Expo's template. There is no `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads`.
- The custom schemes `barry`, `barry-preview` and `barry-dev` have no universal link, so another app could claim one.
- `expo-dev-client` lets a link point development builds at any Metro server.
- Log out and Delete Account leave the offline Check-in queue on the phone. The next account's flush is refused by the server (`not_found`).

**The web:**
- The Trail Map page and the other pages have no `<meta http-equiv="Content-Security-Policy">` and no `<meta name="referrer">`. The token stays in the fragment, which browsers never send. A CSP would contain SEC-5.
- OpenFreeMap sees the contact's IP and the area of the Trail through the tiles it serves, but never the token. `web/privacy.html` discloses it.

**Supply chain and CI:**
- `.github/dependabot.yml` sets `package-ecosystem: ""`, so no update or security pull request is ever raised. This is why SEC-5 went unnoticed.
- `deliver`'s npm dependencies resolve without a committed lock (`deno.lock` is git-ignored), so each deploy may pick up new transitive versions.
- Actions are pinned by tag, not SHA, including the third-party `maxim-lobanov/setup-xcode@v1`, and checkout keeps its credentials.
- `trail-map.yml` gives `pages: write` and `id-token: write` to the job that also runs `npm ci` and `expo export`.
- `package.json` runs `eas-cli@latest` unpinned with the developer's Expo and Apple credentials.
- `scripts/hosted-secrets.mjs` uploads `AUTH_SMTP_PASS` to `deliver`, which does not need it. If its `secrets unset` step fails, the local `TWILIO_API_URL` stays on the hosted project, and every text message, Alerts included, fails until fixed (`delivery_ok` would flag it).
- `scripts/sms-relay.mjs` listens on every interface, so anyone on the same Wi-Fi can read the local test messages.
- `.gitignore` does not cover a plain `.env`, `.env.production` or EAS `credentials.json`.
- `docs/ci-simulator.md` sketches CI with an account-wide `SUPABASE_ACCESS_TOKEN` and a service-role key.
- npm audit: `zod` (high) and `deepmerge` (low) run only in the Expo CLI, prebuild and Jest, never in the app, and SDK 58 has no fix for them.
- `.claude/settings.json` enables the plugin `pstack@pstack-claude`, whose content I could not inspect.

## 5. Checked and safe

1. **Database privileges and RLS:**
   - RLS is on for every exposed table, every policy uses `(select auth.uid())` with a matching `with check`, and the one view is `security_invoker`.
   - The column grants keep `adventurer_id` out of `UPDATE`.
   - Every command takes the Adventurer from `auth.uid()` and refuses others' ids with `not_found`. Probes: Check-in, move, record Trail, delete, Start and save on another's Adventure; contacts of another account; inserts and updates as another account.
   - Service-only functions refuse `anon` and `authenticated`, and the anonymous key reads no table.
   - `search_path = ''` everywhere.
   - `tick` catches a failing Adventure per row, so one bad row cannot stop the clock.
   - Account deletion cascades every table.
2. **Trail Map and token:**
   - 128 bits from `gen_random_bytes(16)`, unique per contact and Adventure.
   - A random token and a removed contact's token both give `null` (probed).
   - No Deadline, contact or id in the answer.
   - The token travels in the fragment and only in a POST body to Supabase.
3. **`deliver`:**
   - Every route is gated (`401` probed without and with a wrong secret), and the callback HMAC binds the message id.
   - Reports can only fail or confirm a text message already sending.
   - Plain-text email only.
   - Outbound hosts are fixed; nothing is built from user data except the recipient.
   - Settlement is lease-checked by `attempts`.
4. **Auth:**
   - Hosted email confirmation is on and anonymous sign-ins are off.
   - Refresh rotation is on; the redirect allow-list is empty, with `site_url` fixed.
   - No account enumeration by sign-up or recovery.
   - Log out and deletion stop the Trail and the geofences and clear the cache.
5. **The app:**
   - Only the public Supabase URL and publishable key are build values; no `console.*`, WebView or data-derived URL.
   - Deep links only open screens; every destructive action needs a confirmation or a hold.
   - Lock-screen texts are as the spec says.
6. **Web:**
   - No `innerHTML`, `eval` or `document.write` with outside data.
   - The `+html.tsx` redirect keeps the path on barry.support.
   - The Pages workflow publishes only the export and `web/`, without source maps.
7. **Secrets:** none in the tree, the history or unreachable objects. The `sb_publishable_…` key in `eas.json` is public by design. `config.toml` and `seed.sql` hold local values only. No secret file is tracked.
8. **Supply chain and CI:**
   - The lockfile is committed with integrity hashes and CI runs `npm ci`.
   - No `pull_request_target`, and `pr-description.yml` passes the title and body through `env`.
   - `ci.yml` is `contents: read` and uses no secrets.
   - The hooks download nothing, and the config plugins touch neither the network nor secrets.
9. **Guides:** none teaches disabling RLS, the service role in the app, a committed secret, `secrets set` by hand or skipping hosted email confirmation.
